# Net Thrust from a Yunshao-Field Gradient in a Closed Cavity: A Decisive Experiment

> **Header (≤300 words)**: This paper does not claim that propellantless thrust has been achieved. It proposes a single experiment that can separate a thermal artefact from a new coupling. The mechanism is the gradient of the Yunshao field: a force density $f=\kappa\,(e^2/2\pi h)\,\nabla\theta\,(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B})$, with one dimensionless coefficient $\kappa$ to be measured. Three independent tests are specified: differential twin cavities, gradient sign reversal, and a constant-absorption frequency sweep at fixed power. A pre-registered falsification line is given. If $\kappa<0.05$ at 95% confidence, the mechanism is rejected for this material class.

---

## Abstract

Cavity-thruster experiments of the EM Drive type reported a net thrust of about $1.2\ \mathrm{mN/kW}$ at NASA Eagleworks in 2016, and were driven to zero by the Tajmar group at TU Dresden in 2021 after thermal expansion, cable forces and residual gas were eliminated. Both data sets are real; the conclusions are opposite. The reason is that no published experiment has measured the one signature that separates the two: a thrust that reverses sign with the material gradient.

We propose a decisive experiment. On the mechanism side we use the gradient coupling of the Yunshao field: the topological angle $\theta$ varies in space, and its gradient multiplies $\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ inside the cavity to produce a force density that can be positive or negative; a uniform $\theta$ gives zero net force. The expression contains one unknown dimensionless coefficient $\kappa$. This paper does not assume its value; it treats $\kappa$ as the single parameter the experiment is designed to measure.

The apparatus uses two cavities of identical geometry, mass and absorbed power. One carries a gradient liner, the other a uniform liner, and they are mounted back to back on a vacuum torsion balance. Three independent checks follow. First, reversing the gradient liner end for end must reverse the thrust, while thermal expansion does not reverse. Second, sweeping frequency at constant absorbed power must collapse the field response along a Lorentzian while the thermal signal stays flat. Third, changing the cavity Q at constant absorbed power must scale the thrust linearly in Q while the thermal signal stays put. If any two of the three fail, the mechanism is falsified.

For $\kappa=1$, a 1 kW cavity with $Q=10^4$ and $0.01\ \mathrm{m^3}$ gives about $32\ \mu\mathrm{N}$, an order of magnitude above the noise floor demonstrated by Tajmar et al. The experiment can therefore choose between "zero" and "natural coupling"; it does not add another unattributable small signal.

**Keywords**: propellantless propulsion; Yunshao field; theta term; axion electrodynamics; torsion balance; pre-registration; artefact control

## 1 The problem: a machine that failed replication

In 2001 Shawyer proposed a conical microwave cavity that would produce net thrust. In 2016 the Eagleworks group at NASA Johnson Space Center published a vacuum torsion-balance measurement reporting $1.2\pm0.1\ \mathrm{mN/kW}$ at 2.45 GHz and about 1 kW input [1]. The Cannae drive, tested in the same period, reported signals of tens of micronewtons.

In 2021 the Tajmar group repeated the measurement with a higher-precision torsion balance. After thermal expansion, cable Lorentz forces and residual-gas convection were removed one by one, the signal went to zero [2]. Their conclusion was that every earlier positive result can be explained by an external force.

The two data sets do not contradict each other: the first did not push thermal effects below the signal, the second did. The question that remains unanswered is this: **if the thrust really comes from a coupling inside the cavity that depends on the material, it should reverse sign when a controllable material property is reversed; if it comes from heat, it will not.** No published experiment has performed that sign-reversal test.

This paper turns that test into an executable experiment.

## 2 Hypothesis: the Yunshao field and the force density

In the literature on topological insulators and axion electrodynamics, the electromagnetic action can carry an extra term [3,4]:

$$
S_\theta=\int d^4x\ \frac{\theta(x)}{2\pi}\,\frac{e^2}{h}\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}.
$$

The Yunshao framework calls the field associated with this term the **Yunshao field**, and writes its topological coupling angle as $\theta$. In the older literature the same object is called the axion field and $\theta$ the axion angle; both names are used when citing that literature. When $\theta$ is constant the term is a total derivative and does not affect the equations of motion; when $\theta$ has a spatial gradient it exerts a force density on the medium. Variation gives

$$
\mathbf{f}=\kappa\,\frac{e^2}{2\pi h}\,\nabla\theta\,(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}),
$$

where $\kappa$ is a dimensionless coefficient and $\kappa=1$ corresponds to "natural coupling", i.e. the topological term entering mechanics at the full $e^2/2\pi h$ strength. Dimensional check: $e^2/h$ is a conductance ($3.874\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{S}$), $\nabla\theta$ is $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, and $\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ is $\mathrm{V\cdot T/m}$; the product is $\mathrm{N/m^3}$, a force density.

This expression is the standard result of axion electrodynamics. Brevik and Chaichian write the force density on the medium as $\mathbf{f}=\rho\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}-g(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B})\nabla\theta$; substituting the medium charge and current and applying one vector identity gives the expression above [6]. $\kappa$ only rescales the standard coefficient: $\kappa=1$ is the standard strength.

Three properties matter:

1. When $\nabla\theta=0$ the net force is zero. No matter how asymmetric the fields inside the cavity are, a uniform $\theta$ integrates to no net force. This does not conflict with momentum conservation: the force acts on the medium, and the medium exchanges momentum with the cavity.
2. The force reverses with $\nabla\theta$. Turn the liner gradient around and the force reverses; thermal expansion always points outward and does not reverse.
3. The force scales with $\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$, hence with cavity Q and absorbed power; a thermal effect scales only with absorbed power.

Two of these properties already have experimental anchors. Wu et al. measured quantized Faraday and Kerr rotation in Bi₂Se₃ films [7]; Dziom et al. measured a Faraday rotation angle equal to the fine-structure constant in a 3D topological insulator [8]; in Weyl semimetals the spatial gradient $\theta(\mathbf{r})=2\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{r}$ is observed through the chiral-anomaly negative magnetoresistance [9]. The theta term and its gradient have both been measured. What has not been measured is whether they give a net thrust to a closed cavity.

The Yunshao framework states these three properties as design rules and explicitly marks the proportionality coefficient as unclosed, with an internal success estimate of 5–10% at system level [5]. This paper keeps that honesty boundary: $\kappa$ is a quantity to be measured, not a known number.

## 3 The decisive experiment: three chains

### 3.1 Apparatus

Two identical cylindrical cavities, inner diameter 0.28 m, length 0.30 m, volume about 0.01 m³, operating in the TM010 mode at 2.45 GHz with an unloaded Q of about $10^4$. The two cavities are mounted back to back on the same torsion rod, with opposite axial orientations so that thermal expansion and radiation pressure cancel to first order.

Cavity A: a removable gradient liner on the inner wall, with $\theta$ running from 0 to $\pi$ along the axis, giving $\nabla\theta\approx\pi/L=10.5\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$.
Cavity B: a liner of identical mass, thickness and absorptivity, but with uniform $\theta$ ($\theta=\pi/2$ everywhere), serving as the "same heat, no gradient" control.
A third liner C is kept on hand: same material, mass and absorptivity as A, with uniform $\theta$, to separate "material" from "gradient".

Each cavity is driven continuously by its own 1 kW magnetron, with both locked to a common regulated supply; the difference in absorbed power is monitored by directional couplers and held below 1%. Runs alternate in one-hour blocks: ten blocks with liner A, then the liner is turned end for end and ten more blocks are taken, with one hour of thermal settling between the two sets. The assembly sits in a $10^{-6}$ Pa vacuum tank with two layers of mu-metal shielding; the torsion balance is read by an optical lever at 10 Hz. Liners are numbered by a third party, and the analyst does not know which orientation a block belongs to until the data are unblinded.

### 3.2 Thrust chain

First measure the differential force between A and B. If the mechanism holds,

$$
F_A-F_B=\kappa\,\frac{e^2}{2\pi h}\,\nabla\theta\,\langle\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}\rangle\,V .
$$

With the numbers of Section 5 this is about $32\ \mu\mathrm{N}$ for $\kappa=1$ and zero for $\kappa=0$. The demonstrated force noise of a torsion balance in the 0.01–1 Hz band is of order $0.3\ \mu\mathrm{N}$ [2], so the signal-to-noise ratio is about 100.

### 3.3 Signature chain

Three independent signatures must all hold:

**Signature one: gradient reversal.** Turn the liner of cavity A end for end, keeping geometry, mass and absorptivity unchanged. $F_A$ must reverse. Thermal expansion, radiation pressure and gas flow do not reverse with the liner. This is the strongest test: it separates "material gradient" from "geometric asymmetry".

**Signature two: constant-absorption frequency sweep.** Sweep from 2.40 to 2.50 GHz in 0.5 MHz steps. At each point adjust the input power so that the net absorbed power stays at 1 kW. If the force comes from the Yunshao-field response it collapses along a Lorentzian with detuning; if it comes from heat, constant absorbed power means constant force. The two curve shapes are distinguishable.

**Signature three: Q scaling.** Change the insertion depth of the coupling probe to move Q from $5\times10^3$ to $2\times10^4$ while keeping absorbed power at 1 kW. The Yunshao-field force grows linearly with Q (because $\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}\propto Q$); a thermal effect does not move with Q. Measure three Q points and take the slope. Radiation pressure also grows with stored energy and therefore with Q; it cancels to first order in the symmetric twin cavity, and to guard against a residual, insert a block with the uniform liner C at the same Q as a zero point.

### 3.4 Ledger chain

Before the first run, the following criteria are written into a pre-registration file with a notarised timestamp:

- Primary criterion: the 95% confidence interval on $\kappa$.
- Falsification line: if $|\kappa|<0.05$, the hypothesis is rejected for this material class; the authors commit to a public retraction and to stopping the line.
- Confirmation line: if $\kappa>0.5$ and all three signatures pass, a second round of independent replication is required, performed by a third party not involved in the first round.
- All data and code are released, including raw torsion waveforms, thermistor records and magnetometer records.
- Momentum ledger: if a net force is measured, strain gauges at the liner mounts must simultaneously read an equal and opposite force; if they do not, that block is discarded.

These three chains of thrust, signature and ledger mirror the three-chain criteria of the Yunshao Qiaofu document [5].

## 4 Artefact list and paired controls

| Artefact | Magnitude | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal expansion | ~50 μm for a 0.3 m copper cavity at 1 kW and 10 K rise | Twin cavities back to back cancel to first order; wait 1 h for thermal steady state; eight thermistors recorded |
| Cable Lorentz force | scales as current squared, up to tens of μN | Fixed cable routing, twisted pairs, coaxial return; measure before and after rewiring |
| Residual gas convection | negligible below $10^{-3}$ Pa | Vacuum better than $10^{-6}$ Pa; pressure scan from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-4}$ Pa |
| Radiation pressure | ~6.7 μN for 1 kW fully reflected | Symmetric cavities cancel; replace with an absorbing load as a control |
| Magnetostriction and geomagnetism | scales as $B^2$ | Two mu-metal layers; remanence below 1 μT |
| Ground vibration | large at low frequency | Torsion mode at 0.2 Hz, away from building peaks; active isolation table |

The purpose of this table is not to list possibilities but to require that every artefact has a paired control that can drive its contribution to zero. If a control cannot be run, that data set does not enter the primary criterion.

## 5 Expected signal and decision

Take $\nabla\theta=\pi/0.3=10.47\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, $V=0.01\ \mathrm{m^3}$, $P=1\ \mathrm{kW}$, $Q=10^4$, $\omega=2\pi\times2.45\ \mathrm{GHz}$:

Energy density
$$
u=\frac{PQ}{\omega V}=6.50\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{J/m^3},
$$
electric field
$$
E=\sqrt{2u/\varepsilon_0}=1.21\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{V/m},
$$
magnetic field $B=E/c=4.04\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{T}$, hence
$$
\langle\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}\rangle=48.9\ \mathrm{V\cdot T/m},
$$
force density
$$
f=6.166\times10^{-6}\times10.47\times48.9=3.16\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{N/m^3},
$$
total force
$$
F=fV=3.16\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{N}=31.6\ \mu\mathrm{N}\quad(\kappa=1).
$$

At $Q=10^5$ and the same absorbed power, $F$ rises to about 316 μN. This range falls exactly in the window where thermal artefacts can be pushed below 1 μN and a torsion balance can still resolve the signal, so the experiment has decision power.

For scale: $31.6\ \mu\mathrm{N}$ on a one-tonne payload gives $3.2\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, about 1 m/s per year. It is not a usable engine; it is an instrument that can answer "whether".

## 6 Relation to earlier experiments

Converting the NASA 2016 value of $1.2\ \mathrm{mN/kW}$ into the present $\kappa$ at their $Q\approx10^4$ gives about 38. If this experiment measures $\kappa\approx1$–$38$, the old data acquire a testable explanation; if it measures $\kappa<0.05$, the old data can only be artefacts and the present hypothesis is closed for this material class. Either outcome is an advance over the current standoff.

To be explicit: this paper does not claim that the NASA data are correct, nor that the Tajmar attribution is wrong. It claims only that between the two explanations there exists a cheap, decisive experiment that no one has yet performed.

## 7 Boundaries and commitments

1. The coefficient $\kappa$ is unclosed. What is given here is "what you would see if $\kappa=1$", not "you will see 32 μN".
2. A defect array with 0.125 nm period has no public fabrication or replication record. This paper treats the material as part of the experiment, not as an established fact.
3. The internal success estimate is 5–10% at system level (Yunshao framework, 2026-09-30) [5]. The number is not flattering, but it is the number the authors are willing to sign.
4. If any two of the three signatures fail, the authors commit to publishing the falsification within 30 days and to stopping the line, rather than extending it on the grounds that "more parameters are needed".
5. This paper does not seek funding. It seeks one independent measurement executed against the criteria above. Any institution that performs Sections 3 and 4 will be accepted, whatever the result.
6. This proposal does not explain where the momentum goes. If the net force is real, the momentum must be carried by the Yunshao field or by the medium; the experiment checks both a strain-gauge channel and a cavity-displacement channel, and a mismatch is scored as an artefact. This is the same rule as the momentum ledger in Section 3.4, written twice because it is the easiest one to skip.

## 8 Conclusion

Yunshao-field gradient coupling gives a force density that can change sign, reverses with the material, and scales with Q. These three properties make it experimentally separable from thermal artefacts. The differential twin cavity, gradient reversal, constant-power sweep and Q scaling described here cost on the order of one million RMB and about 18 months, and have enough decision power to close or open the route.

If the result is zero, we lose one experiment and one hypothesis. If it is not zero, we gain an instrument that can measure a new coupling. Either outcome is worth more than another argument about a 2016 machine.
